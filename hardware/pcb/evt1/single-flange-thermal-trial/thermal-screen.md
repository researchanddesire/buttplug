# Compass EVT thermistor PCB thermal isolation

A local ground-pour cut removes a path through copper. A routed relief with a thin laminate neck controls the remaining heat path through the board. At each NTCS0402E3103FL1T, retain two independent sense wires and keep the high-current and RF reference planes intact outside the local relief. The active flange stack specifies a 1 mm finished PCB, nominal 35 µm copper on all four layers, 0.15 mm traces and conductor spacing, and 0.30 mm copper-to-edge clearance. Check foil and finished plating with the fab because greater copper thickness increases thermal conduction. At the proposed 3.352 mm PCB-to-pouch gap, a 0.55 mm installed thermistor leaves 2.802 mm over the chip. The full 36 mm² pad-area assumption with 1.0 W/(m·K) conductivity and 20 K/W combined interface resistance gives a pouch-to-sensor thermal resistance of 97.8 K/W. The settled board fraction in the two-path model is `f = R_pouch / (R_pouch + R_board)`. The bench allocation of ≤1.5 °C error with a 20 °C board-to-pouch difference permits `f ≤ 0.075`, which gives `R_board ≥ 1207` K/W and ≤0.829 mW/K total warm-side conductance at nominal pad contact. The 2.68 °C guard cold allowance before uncertainty and shutdown alone would give ≥632 K/W. Treat 1.5 °C as a ceiling for every harmful error contribution combined. The coupon needs margin for interface spreading, incomplete contact, sensor self-heating and actual transient lag. [Henkel TGP 1000VOUS data sheet](https://datasheets.tdx.henkel.com/BERGQUIST-GAP-PAD-TGP-1000VOUS-en_GL.pdf)

For the plane-keepout control, the screening model treats the uninterrupted laminate as a 1 to 4 mm radial annulus. Using 1 mm thickness and representative `k = 0.4` W/(m·K) gives `ln(r_o / r_i) / (2π k t) = 552` K/W before traces. Two 4 mm traces, each 0.15 × 0.035 mm², conduct in parallel and lower that example to 353 K/W. Radial dimensions and conductivity are illustrative. The condition of the real stackup controls the measured result. For a routed tab, use `G = (k_FR4 × w × t + 2 × k_Cu × w_Cu × t_Cu) / L`. A 1.20 mm wide neck houses two 0.15 mm traces, 0.15 mm between them and 0.30 mm to either routed edge with 0.15 mm to distribute as manufacturing design room. A 6 mm neck is a useful test, with 8 mm or longer preferable where the clearances allow it. One 8 mm sense conductor of these dimensions adds roughly 0.03 Ω of electrical series resistance. Each thermistor needs its own signal and return wiring. Preserve independent returns to the body J2.2S. Avoid vias, power or ground fill, or any additional copper across the neck. The air-path example assumes 20 mm of 1 mm high facing edge, 0.8 mm separation, and 0.0264 W/(m·K) air. This one-dimensional path contributes 0.66 mW/K if exposed to the full local gradient. The actual temperature field can reduce or increase the assembly leakage. An 8 mm neck already contributes 0.572 mW/K under nominal assumptions, leaving roughly 0.257 mW/K for warm-side parasitics. Routing clearance and the length of directly opposed board edges matter as much as neck length once this budget is small. Insulating mount points and a suitably shaped slot can reduce thermal leakage. An air gap can still exchange heat by conduction and radiation. A thermal pad that overhangs the island onto warm board can bypass the isolation. For example, 2 mm² of bridge cross section over 2 mm of this silicone conducts 1 mW/K, more than the whole warm-side budget. Witness the actual pad edges at the largest compression and displacement, including adhesive and silicone. Under volume conservation, equal spreading in both axes grows a 6.0 mm square to approximately 6.93 mm at 25% compression or 7.17 mm at 30%. This sizing check gives approximately 7.4–7.7 mm overall after a provisional ±0.25 mm placement allowance. Real side bulge and wet area require measurement. If mechanical clearance drives a narrower pad or sensor-side pickup, its reduced area raises pouch thermal resistance. At half nominal pad contact the screening model roughly doubles `R_pouch` and the required warm-side resistance. Keep pad load off the chip body and solder joints, and carry restraint forces through the rigid tray. A load stop or support underneath an island needs its own thermal path assessment. Compare an uncut local plane keepout with routed relief on an inert fit coupon using the same two NTC types, pads, closure and core stack. Instrument the nearby warm motherboard, each NTC, each pouch contact point and nearby case or air. Verify PCB temperature near the source side of the isolation and at or near the island with a small reference that does not bridge the gap. At true pouch 0 °C, step PCB to 20 °C and measure harmful error and charge inhibition. Reverse the heat-source gradient with true 45 °C pouch and PCB at 35 °C, then repeat near both boundaries at the credible thermal ramp rate and after minimum pad compression, closure motion and dwell. Use the same ≤1.5 °C thermal error, ≤0.3 °C expanded measurement uncertainty, and ≤0.4 °C pouch motion during cutoff screen. Confirm loss of one or both interfaces electrically intact, holding the other channel in range where needed to demonstrate each cutoff. Isolation reduces warm-board influence with valid thermal contact. A detached sensor can still return a plausible value, so this board trial does not by itself justify qualifying the PCB channels as the sole temperature protection.
